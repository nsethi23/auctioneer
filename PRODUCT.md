# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Auctioneer is for technically minded users who want to inspect how sponsored-search auction mechanisms behave. The primary users are the project owner, interviewers, reviewers, students, and engineers evaluating mechanism design, game theory, and learning behavior through concrete simulations.

Users arrive with a question like "how does GSP differ from VCG?", "does bid shading improve utility?", or "did the learning agent converge toward the analytical best response?" They need the interface to make the auction mechanics visible, not hide them behind generic charts.

## Product Purpose

Auctioneer turns ad-auction theory into testable experiments. It compares GSP and VCG, bidder strategies, best-response behavior, Nash-style dynamics, and Q-learning convergence through reproducible inputs and measurable outputs.

Success means a user can run an experiment, inspect allocations and payments, compare revenue, welfare, and surplus, and explain the economic result from the data shown on screen.

## Brand Personality

Precise, rigorous, calm.

The product should feel like a focused research workbench: confident enough for interview demos, but restrained enough that the math remains the center of attention. The voice should be direct, specific, and evidence-led.

## Anti-references

Auctioneer should not look like a marketing splash page, crypto dashboard, stock-trading terminal, or generic SaaS metrics grid. Avoid oversized hero sections, decorative gradients, neon-on-black palettes, cartoon game styling, vague AI-demo language, and charts that imply certainty without showing assumptions.

Avoid hiding mechanism details behind a single score. The product should expose inputs, allocations, payments, utility, welfare, uncertainty, and convergence evidence.

## Design Principles

1. Make the mechanism visible. Ranking, slot assignment, payment, utility, and welfare should be inspectable without digging.
2. Compare before summarizing. GSP, VCG, Nash, and learned behavior should be shown side by side when the user is making a claim.
3. Evidence over polish. Visual emphasis should serve computed results, not decoration.
4. Preserve mathematical legibility. Use precise labels, tabular numbers, stable layouts, and explicit assumptions.
5. Support interview explanation. Screens should help the user narrate what happened and why it matters.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and interactive controls. Do not rely on color alone for positive or negative outcomes. Keep dense tables readable with clear labels, stable alignment, keyboard-accessible controls, and visible focus states. Motion should be subtle and avoid blocking interpretation of results.
